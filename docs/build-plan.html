01 — What your reference is actually made of

The design read

Before any code, here's what I pulled out of the board so we're building against something specific rather than a vibe.

Palette

#5C1220Deep wine — hover states, card borders, depth
#F3E6CECream — card fills, the light panels
#A89574Khaki — the page ground everything floats on
#C79A5BGilt — accent, borrowed from the gilded interiors
#2A1A16Ink — body copy, warm rather than neutral black

Sample these off the image yourself before locking them — screen colour picks drift. These are close, not gospel.

Layout system

Grid

Asymmetric bento

Cards of unequal span on a 12-column grid — a wide hero, a tall portrait, small square stat blocks. Not a uniform card wall.

Radius

Two-tier rounding

Big containers sit around 24–28px; nested elements around 12–16px. Keeping the ratio consistent is what makes it read as designed.

Depth

Picture-in-picture insets

Small framed images overlapping larger ones, plus floating profile badges. This overlap is where the parallax will live.

Metaphor

Social profile as chrome

Follower counts, tab bars, “Edit profile” buttons — the UI of a social account borrowed as the frame for your actual work.

One honest flag on the concept

The social-profile framing is striking, but it's a frame, not the content. A hiring manager or client landing here needs to reach your actual work in one scroll. Keep the profile chrome as the wrapper, and make sure real projects — with images, roles, outcomes — sit inside it rather than being implied by follower counts. That's the difference between a portfolio and a moodboard.

02 — Tooling

The stack

Everything below is free, has strong docs, and is what people building motion-heavy sites actually reach for. Nothing here needs a server you pay for.

Framework

Next.js — App Router

React with routing, image optimisation, and font loading built in. Deploys to Vercel in one click.

Over plain Vite/React: next/image and next/font alone save you hours, and your portfolio will be image-heavy.

Language

TypeScript

Catches typos in your project data before the page breaks. Set to a loose config so it helps without nagging.

Since you're learning as you go, the editor autocomplete is worth more than the type safety.

Styling

Tailwind CSS v4

Your palette gets defined once in a @theme block, then used as bg-wine, text-cream everywhere.

Over CSS Modules: for a design this token-driven, having the palette in one place you can tweak is the whole game.

Motion — core

Motion for React

The library formerly called Framer Motion. Handles reveals, hover physics, and the shared-layout transitions between your account tabs.

Its layoutId feature is the single reason your nav pill and tab switching will feel expensive.

Motion — scroll

Lenis

Smooth inertial scrolling. Small, and it's what gives scroll-linked parallax that weighted, non-janky feel.

Optional but transformative here. Add it in phase 3 and you'll immediately feel the difference.

Motion — timeline

GSAP + ScrollTrigger

Only if you want a complex pinned scroll sequence later. Fully free now, including ScrollTrigger.

Don't install this on day one. Motion covers 90% of what you need; reach for GSAP when you hit the 10%.

Icons

Lucide React

Clean line icons matching the home / search / menu glyphs in your reference.

Tree-shakeable, so unused icons cost nothing.

Hosting

Vercel

Free tier, connects to GitHub, redeploys on every push, gives you a preview URL per branch.

Custom domain is roughly $10–15/yr and worth it — yourname.com reads better than a subdomain.

Typography

Font pairing

Your reference uses a geometric sans throughout. Load via next/font/google so there's no layout shift. Two directions worth trying: Instrument Serif for display paired with Geist for body gives it an editorial, gallery-catalogue feel that suits the museum imagery. Or stay all-sans with Bricolage Grotesque for headings and Inter for body if you want closer fidelity to the board.

03 — The part you called “dynamic”

Motion, mapped to your design

Not generic animation advice — each of these is tied to a specific element visible in your reference board.

Nav pill indicator

The wine pill holding your four account tabs. Give the active-tab highlight a shared layoutId — Motion then physically slides it between tabs instead of cutting. Roughly six lines of code, and it's the first thing people notice.

Bento entrance

Cards rise and fade as they enter the viewport, staggered ~60ms apart. Use whileInView with viewport={{ once: true }} so it doesn't re-fire on every scroll-back — that re-firing is what makes sites feel cheap.

Hero parallax

The small framed inset over the large portrait. Drive both from useScroll at different useTransform rates so the inset drifts against the backdrop. Keep the offset under ~40px — subtle reads as expensive, large reads as a template.

Stat count-up

Your 337 / 687 / 1,373 numbers ticking up from zero when scrolled into view. Use useMotionValue + animate, and set font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums or the digits will jitter the layout as they change width.

Card tilt on hover

Profile cards tilting toward the cursor via rotateX/rotateY mapped from pointer position, wrapped in useSpring for weight. Cap the rotation around 6–8 degrees. Gate it behind a pointer check so it never fires on touch.

Tab transitions

Your showpiece. Switching between the Facebook / Instagram / TikTok / Pinterest views morphs the shared elements — avatar, name, container — rather than swapping panels. Motion's AnimatePresence plus layoutId on the persistent pieces.

Image reveals

Gallery images wiping in under an animated clip-path instead of a plain fade. Suits the museum photography and costs nothing in performance.

Reduced motion

Not optional. Wrap everything in a useReducedMotion check so animations collapse to instant states for anyone who's asked their OS for that. This is an accessibility requirement, and it's five minutes of work if you build it in from phase 3 rather than retrofitting.

04 — Sequence

Build phases

Genuinely ordered — each phase depends on the one before it. The big rule: the entire site works and looks right before a single animation exists. Motion is a layer you add on top of something finished, never a thing you build alongside layout.

00

Foundation

Scaffold Next.js with TypeScript and Tailwind, initialise git, push to GitHub, connect Vercel. You'll have a live URL before you've written any of your own code.

Done when  pushing to main updates a real public URL.

01

Tokens and type

Palette into a Tailwind @theme block, fonts wired through next/font, the radius and spacing scale set. Build one throwaway page that shows every colour and text size together.

Done when  you can change one hex value and watch the whole site shift.

02

Static layout, real content

Every section built with zero animation and your actual work in it — real project images, real copy, real bio. Responsive from 360px up. This is the longest phase and the one that decides whether the site is any good.

Done when  it's genuinely shippable as-is, just still.

03

Motion foundations

Install Motion and Lenis. Build one reusable <Reveal> component with the reduced-motion guard baked in, and apply it everywhere. Resist adding anything custom yet.

Done when  the whole page reveals coherently and OS reduced-motion turns it all off.

04

Signature interactions

The nav pill indicator, bento stagger, hero parallax, stat count-ups, card tilt. Add one at a time and deploy after each — that way you always know which change broke something.

Done when  the page feels alive without feeling busy.

05

The tab transition

Shared-layout morphing between the four account views. Hardest piece, highest payoff — which is why it comes after you're comfortable with Motion's basics rather than first.

Done when  switching tabs morphs rather than cuts.

06

Polish and ship

Images converted to WebP and sized properly, Lighthouse run, keyboard navigation checked, focus states visible, alt text written, page metadata and social preview image set, custom domain pointed.

Done when  Lighthouse performance and accessibility both clear 90.

06 — Deliberately not building

Deferred, with triggers

You ruled these out, and I agree for a v1 — but here's the honest signal for when each one starts earning its complexity.

A CMS

Add Sanity or Contentlayer when editing projects.ts starts feeling like a chore, or when you want to post from your phone. Until then a data file is faster and free.

Live social APIs

Realistically, probably never. Instagram and TikTok both require app review, tokens that expire, and a business account — for numbers that change slowly. Hardcode the counts in profile.ts and update them when you remember.

Contact form

Add a Next.js route handler plus Resend when you actually want inbound messages. For v1, a mailto: link converts just as well and takes thirty seconds.

Blog / writing

MDX when you have three posts already written. Setting up a blog before you have anything to put in it is the most reliable way to stall a portfolio.
